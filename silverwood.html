Your panels, your roof, your spinners

What spinners cover, and what they don't

They do
  • Flash sunlight and keep moving in the wind. Pigeons stay away from light and motion they can't predict.
  • Keep birds off the open roof, the ridge and the eaves, where they land and walk.
  • Cover the side of the roof they face. The flash travels in a straight line.
  • Mount on your roof vents with stainless hose clamps. No drilling into the roof.
They don't
  • Clear a nest that's already under your panels. The mesh does that.
  • See over the ridge. One spinner on the front leaves the back side open, and the pigeons just move there.
  • Replace the mesh. Mesh is the guarantee, spinners are the deterrent.

Why Silverwood homes get dirty faster than the rest of Hesperia

Silverwood is the largest master planned community being built in Southern California: 15,633 homes on 9,366 acres in Hesperia's Summit Valley, going up in phases over the next fifteen to twenty years. That is good news for the neighborhood and hard news for your glass. Every lot graded near you puts fine dust in the air, and on a High Desert afternoon it settles on the windows and the solar panels within days.

New homes also start with their own mess. Builders clean the glass before closing, but stucco specks, paint overspray and sticker residue usually survive it, and builder screens trap the grit against the pane. The first proper clean is the one people notice most.

Then there is the roof. California has required solar on new single family homes since 2020, so most Silverwood homes came with panels on day one. A dusty panel still looks fine from the driveway while its output drifts down, and with construction nearby it gets dusty fast.

We treat Silverwood as its own place, with its own page and its own schedule, because the work here is different from an older street across town.

FAQ

Silverwood questions

I scanned the door hanger. Are these the real prices?

Yes. Exterior windows are $149 for a single story and $249 for a two story, with screens, tracks and sills included. Every interior window is $49 more on the same visit. Solar panels are $7 each. Those are the same numbers on every page of this site, and you get a firm price in writing before any work starts.

Do you actually come out to Silverwood?

Yes, and there is no trip fee. We are based in Hesperia, a short drive from Silverwood, and we are working the community this week. Text or call and we can usually get you on the schedule within a day or two.

My home is brand new. Does it really need cleaning already?

Most new homes do. Builders clean the glass before closing, but stucco specks, paint overspray and label residue are often still on it, and the neighborhood keeps building around you. Every lot being graded nearby puts fine dust in the air, and it settles on the windows and the solar panels. The first clean is usually the most noticeable one.

Can cleaning damage my new solar panels or their warranty?

We clean panels the gentle way: purified water and a soft brush on a water fed pole. No pressure washer, no harsh chemicals, and nobody walking on the panels. If your panels are leased or under a provider's service plan, check your agreement first, and we are happy to work around it.

How often should Silverwood homes be cleaned?

While the lots around you are still being built, dust comes back quickly. Most people here are happiest with windows every three to four months and solar panels twice a year. After the construction near you finishes, twice a year for windows is usually enough.

Does the association have rules about contractors?

We follow any rules posted by the association, work during normal daytime hours and leave the driveway and walkways clean. Nothing we do changes the look of the home. If your association or builder wants a certificate of insurance before we start, we send one.

What if I am not happy with a window?

Tell us before we leave and we redo it on the spot. That is the satisfaction guarantee, and it covers every pane we clean.
