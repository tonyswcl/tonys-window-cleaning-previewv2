Why It Matters

Pigeons under solar panels aren't just noisy

The gap between your panels and the roof is warm, shaded and hidden from hawks. Out here it's the best real estate a pigeon will ever find — and once a pair nests, the flock follows.

  • ⚡ Your panels stop producing what you're paying for Droppings and nesting debris shade cells and bake on in 100°+ heat. A fouled array doesn't produce the numbers you financed it on.
  • 🧱 Droppings eat roofing and hardware Pigeon droppings are acidic. Left on tile, shingle, flashing and panel mounts, they corrode and stain — and the damage isn't reversible with a rinse.
  • 🔥 Nesting material is dry tinder next to live wiring Twigs, feathers and dried droppings packed against conduit and junction boxes are exactly what you don't want on a High Desert roof in August.
  • 🐾 It ends up in your yard, your pool and your dog Every rain and every wind moves droppings off the roof and down. Pets that get into it get sick, and pool chemistry pays for it too.
  • 😷 Dried droppings turn into airborne dust This is why we sanitize instead of sweeping, and why we bag and haul everything. Dry-brushing a fouled array just puts it in the air you breathe.
  • 📈 It gets worse, never better Pigeons return to a successful nest site and breed year-round in this climate. The job is always cheaper before the flock triples.

Know Your Enemy

Why pigeons choose solar panels

The gap between a solar panel and the roof runs about four to six inches. It's shaded from the sun, sheltered from wind, warmed from above by the panel and from below by the roof — and almost nothing can reach a bird once it's in there. If you set out to design a nesting site for a pigeon, you'd build exactly that.

This is why the problem is so common on newer homes. Rooftop solar became standard on California new construction, and nobody installing it was thinking about birds. A neighborhood built in the last fifteen years is a neighborhood full of ideal nesting sites, and pigeons are very good at finding them.

They also don't leave. Pigeons return to the same nesting site season after season, and their offspring come back to the site they were raised in. That's why hosing it out or chasing them off buys a few months at most. Until the gap is physically closed, you have a permanent tenant.

The Alternatives, Honestly

What about spikes, netting, gel and spinners?

Fair question — here's the straight answer on each.

  • 📌 Spikes — right product, wrong place Spikes deter birds from landing on a flat open surface like a ledge or parapet. They do nothing about the gap under your panels, because the birds aren't landing there — they're walking in from the side.
  • 🕸️ Netting — built for commercial, not your roof Netting works on large commercial structures where the whole area gets enclosed. On a residential array it looks poor, sags in the wind, and traps debris.
  • 🧴 Gels and repellents — temporary by design They degrade in UV and need reapplication — and desert sun is unusually hard on them. You'd be buying the same fix twice a year.
  • ✨ Reflective spinners — a real supplement, not a solution Spinners discourage birds from settling on the open roof around the array, which reduces pressure on the barrier. They don't close the gap. We include them with most jobs for exactly that supporting role.
  • 🔒 Physical exclusion — the only permanent answer Close the gap and there's nothing to argue about. That's why mesh secured with clips is the core of every job we do, backed by our 2-year pigeon-free warranty.
Timing

When to do it

Any time is better than waiting — but fall and winter are easiest. Pigeons nest in spring, roughly February through April. Exclusion done in autumn means they never establish for the coming season. Wait until April and we're removing active nests with eggs or chicks in them, which is slower, messier and harder on the birds. Reading this in the fall? This is the right time.

FAQ

Pigeon proofing questions

How much does pigeon proofing cost?

Most solar array jobs start at $450 and are priced by panel count and roof access. That includes removing old nests and mesh, full dropping cleanup and sanitizing, and installing new mesh secured with clips. The inspection is free and the quote is firm before we start.

Why do you use clips instead of bolts?

Drilling bolts through the mesh puts holes in the material and, on some installs, in the panel frame — which can void your solar warranty. Worse, the mesh works itself off those bolts in High Desert wind and the pigeons walk right back in. Stainless clips grip the panel frame without penetrating it, hold in wind, and come off cleanly if a panel ever needs service.

Do you remove the droppings and nests too?

Yes — and it's included, not an upcharge. We remove the nests, bag and haul the debris, wash and sanitize the affected roof and panel area, then rinse the panels. You should never be left with cleanup after a pigeon proofing job.

Is it humane?

Yes. We don't trap, poison or harm the birds. We remove the nesting habitat, seal the gap under the array, and add deterrents so the flock relocates and stops treating your roof as home.

How long does it take?

Most residential solar arrays are finished in a single day. Larger arrays, heavy nesting or difficult roof access can run into a second day — and we tell you that at the inspection, not after we've started.

What if the pigeons come back?

Our pigeon proofing carries a 2-year pigeon-free warranty. If pigeons get back under the array within that period, we come back and fix it at no charge.

Will the mesh void my solar warranty?

No. We clip to the panel frame and never drill into panels, penetrate the roof or touch the electrical system. If your installer requires notification, tell us and we'll document the work for them.

Can I just clean it out myself?

We'd rather you didn't. Dried droppings and nesting dust are a respiratory hazard, and the work happens on a roof, around live DC wiring, often on a slope. It's also only half the job if the gap stays open.

What if my panels are leased or under warranty?

Exclusion work of this kind is generally fine, but check your agreement. We'll provide photos and a description of the work for your provider.
