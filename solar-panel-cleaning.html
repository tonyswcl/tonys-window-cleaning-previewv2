Your panels, your roof, your spinners

What spinners cover, and what they don't

They do
  • Flash sunlight and keep moving in the wind. Pigeons stay away from light and motion they can't predict.
  • Keep birds off the open roof, the ridge and the eaves, where they land and walk.
  • Cover the side of the roof they face. The flash travels in a straight line.
  • Mount on your roof vents with stainless hose clamps. No drilling into the roof.
They don't
  • Clear a nest that's already under your panels. The mesh does that.
  • See over the ridge. One spinner on the front leaves the back side open, and the pigeons just move there.
  • Replace the mesh. Mesh is the guarantee, spinners are the deterrent.
Why It Matters Out Here

The desert gives you sun, then covers it in dust

There's a reason solar panel cleaning matters more in the High Desert than at the coast. Wind pushes fine grit off open land and unpaved roads, it settles on your glass, and unlike wetter parts of the state, we get almost no rain to wash it off again.

What makes it easy to ignore is that a dirty panel looks fine from the ground. Nothing breaks, no light comes on, no alarm goes off. Output just drifts downward month over month, and unless you're watching your production data, you won't notice. Most homeowners we clean for have never opened their inverter app.

How much output dust actually costs is worth being honest about. Independent research consistently finds soiling reduces production, and the size of the loss depends on how dirty the panels are and how long since the last rain. Low single-digit percentages are normal for lightly soiled panels; heavy buildup, bird droppings or a full season of desert dust push it substantially higher. We won't quote a specific number for your roof, because we'd be guessing. Check your own production history after a cleaning and the panels will tell you themselves.

One more thing we find constantly: about half the arrays we open up in this region have birds living under them, and that's usually news to the owner. It's why every cleaning includes an inspection, and why solar cleaning and pigeon proofing are so often the same visit.

How We Clean

The method matters as much as the visit

Panel glass has an anti-reflective coating. The wrong approach damages it permanently.

Purified, deionized water

Tap water out here is hard. It dries into mineral spots that block light, which defeats the point. Our water is filtered and deionized so it dries with nothing left behind. No soap, no detergent, no residue for the next round of dust to stick to.

Soft brushes, water-fed poles

Never abrasive pads, never harsh chemicals, never a pressure washer. The coating on your glass stays intact and the warranty stays safe.

Full inspection, every visit

Glass, frames, mounting hardware and what's going on underneath the array. If something looks wrong we photograph it and tell you, including the birds that half these roofs are hiding.

Timing

How often should panels be cleaned?

  • 🗓️ Twice a year for most High Desert homes Spring after the windy season, fall before the shorter days do the work. That rhythm keeps output steady without over-servicing.
  • 🛻 More often near dust sources Unpaved roads, active construction, agricultural land, or birds on the roof. Any of those and quarterly starts paying for itself.
  • 🏡 Once a year for sheltered properties Newer surrounding development and paved streets slow the buildup. The first clean after a long gap always takes longest.

FAQ

Solar cleaning questions

How much does solar panel cleaning cost?

Cleaning starts at $7 per panel. Standard rates in the High Desert run $10–$12 a panel, so we sit below market for the same professional purified-water method. Already booking pigeon proofing? Panel cleaning bundles at a lower rate since we're on the roof anyway.

Will you damage my panels?

No. Soft brush, purified water, water-fed pole. No pressure washing, no abrasives, no chemicals. Panel glass has an anti-reflective coating and we treat it accordingly.

Can I just hose my solar panels off?

It moves dust around and leaves hard water spots that block light. Tap water out here is mineral heavy and it dries onto the glass. Our water is filtered and deionized so it dries with nothing left behind.

Should I clean my solar panels myself?

Roof work has real risk and panels are easy to damage. If you do, use soft materials, no pressure, and go up early in the morning when the glass is cool. Wet panels in full sun can crack from thermal shock.

How often should solar panels be cleaned in the High Desert?

Twice a year suits most High Desert homes: spring after the windy season, fall before the shorter days. More often near unpaved roads, construction, agricultural land, or if you have birds on the roof.

Do you clean ground mount arrays?

Yes, and they are usually quicker and cheaper since there is no roof access involved.

How do I know if my panels need cleaning?

Look at your production data over the last few months and compare to the same period last year. If output is drifting down without a good reason, that's your answer.
